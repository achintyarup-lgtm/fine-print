More than **five and a half lakh** free cooking-gas connections under the Ujjwala scheme went to homes that already had a gas connection, the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) has found.

**The scheme is meant only for poor households that do not have LPG.** The CAG estimates that these connections put a burden of **₹326.61 crore** on public money, counting the connections and the free refills, subsidies and safety checks that came with them.

The auditor also found **1.64 lakh** cases of the same family getting more than one connection, and **2.61 lakh** connections meant for the poorest families going to others. It says Aadhaar details of all adults in a family were often not entered, which made such cases harder to catch.

The oil companies told the auditor they had decided to close the old connections and keep the Ujjwala ones, so that no poor family lost its free connection. The details, and what the government and the companies told the auditor, are below.

## What is Ujjwala?

The Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana is a central government scheme. It gives women from poor families an LPG connection for cooking. It is meant only for homes that do not already have one.

## Who checked it?

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) checks how the government spends public money. Its report on Ujjwala was placed in Parliament on 12 August 2026.

## What did the CAG find?

- **Homes that already had gas:** **5,50,446** Ujjwala connections went to homes that already had an ordinary gas connection. **The CAG says this was against the scheme’s rules.**
- **Double connections:** When the oil companies checked again at the CAG’s request, they found **1,63,943** cases of the same person or family getting more than one connection. **12,718** of these got through even though the companies’ checks were in place.
- **Poorest families’ share:** **2,61,169** connections reserved for the poorest families, those with an Antyodaya ration card, went to families with other ration cards. Uttar Pradesh had the most (**1,44,115**), then West Bengal (**88,942**), Uttarakhand (13,272), Gujarat (10,886) and Andhra Pradesh (3,954).
- **Connections in men’s names:** **2,141** connections were in men’s names, though the scheme is for women.

## How much money is involved?

The CAG estimates that the 5.5 lakh connections to homes that already had gas put a burden of **₹326.61 crore** on public money. This includes the cost of the connections and benefits meant only for eligible families, such as free refills during Covid, the refill subsidy and safety checks.

By company: HPCL **₹194.10 crore**, IOCL **₹92.80 crore** and BPCL **₹39.71 crore**. The double connections put a further burden of **₹40.82 crore**, the CAG says.

## Why did the checks fail?

To catch double connections, the Aadhaar numbers of all adults in a family were supposed to be entered. The CAG found that **4.28 crore of 4.91 crore connections (87 per cent)** were given using the Aadhaar of just one or two family members. The oil companies had made their own rule in March 2018 allowing this. **The CAG says it went against the petroleum ministry’s instructions.**

A government office that checks the oil companies’ bills, the Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell (PPAC), found **4.07 lakh** double connections. It refused to pay **₹65.10 crore** of the companies’ claims. When the CAG checked, the companies had shut only **3,315** of these connections.

## Loans that may not come back

The oil companies also lent poor families **₹9,552.99 crore** without interest. The money is recovered from the subsidy on later refills. By March 2023, **less than half (46.31 per cent)** had come back. The CAG warns that many families buy few refills, so the rest may not be recovered. The ministry says **₹4,968.27 crore** was still due in September 2024.

## What the government and the oil companies say

- **On the 5.5 lakh connections:** The oil companies told the auditor (January/February 2024) that they had decided to block or close the old connection and keep the Ujjwala one, so that no poor family lost its free connection. When the audit was done, the ministry had not yet replied on this.
- **On double connections:** The companies said mistakes “cannot be ruled out” because their computer systems kept changing, and that the cases were being checked. The ministry said these were only 0.3 per cent of all LPG connections.
- **Later action:** In replies between April 2024 and January 2025, the ministry said 15,812 of the connections found by PPAC had been shut and 58,009 had been “regularised” after checking. The CAG says the rest still need action.
- **On the poorest families’ share:** The ministry said ration-card records were not all online when the scheme began, so gas distributors accepted paper cards. It said it checked 23,205 such connections and found 23,157 genuine.
- **On connections in men’s names:** The companies said most are cases where the woman died and the connection passed to a male relative. The rules allow this if no other adult woman in the family is alive. The ministry said 2,094 were genuine, 24 were blocked and the rest were being checked. The CAG says the auditors were not shown documents such as death certificates.

## What happens now?

The CAG has asked for three things: Aadhaar numbers of all adults in the family, online checks of ration cards before a connection is given, and recovery of money wrongly paid.

*The replies of the petroleum ministry and the oil companies in this story are as recorded in the CAG report.*
